# DECISION MEMO — S61 PAYMENT-REVERSAL ACCOUNTING (the STOP subsets)

**Session:** ERP S61 · **Status of the memo'd items:** POLICY-BLOCKED / DEFERRED — the governed payment reversal (D4) and the financial delete boundary (D6) are IMPLEMENTED for the defined, derivable case (see `SESSION61-PAYMENT-REVERSAL-CERTIFICATION.md`). This memo records the two boundaries the operator's own STOP conditions reserved, so nothing undefined was guessed.

## 1 · Bank-reconciled payment reversal — POLICY-BLOCKED (operator-flagged STOP)

- **Operator instruction (verbatim intent):** "A bank-reconciled payment must not have its reconciliation evidence silently erased. If reversal of a reconciled payment requires a separate reconciliation state or authority that does not exist: STOP and memo. Do not weaken the S55 bankReconciledAt protection."
- **What S61 did:** the reversal `validate` REFUSES an original payment that carries a stored `bankReconciledAt` (a finalized bank statement vouches for it as settled fact). The refusal never touches `bankReconciledAt` — S55 is not weakened; the payment stays immutable and bank-evidenced. Pinned (`session61PaymentReversal.test.ts` — "refuses reversing a BANK-RECONCILED payment").
- **Why STOP (what is undefined):** reversing a payment that a finalized bank statement already matched is a bank-side correction. It needs a **reconciliation-reversal state + authority** that the repository does not model: does reversing un-match the statement line? re-open the statement? require a distinct bank-reconciliation-manager authority? None of these exist. Inventing them is inventing accounting + authority — forbidden.
- **Minimum information needed to unblock:** (1) the bank-correction record model (does a reversal of a reconciled payment produce a bank-adjustment entry, or must the statement be re-opened first?); (2) the authority that may perform it (a bank-reconciliation role, or finance-admin — shares the D8–D11 approval-control-plane question); (3) whether `bankReconciledAt`/`bankStatementRef` are cleared, preserved, or superseded. Then one bounded slice implements bank-reconciled reversal on those semantics.
- **Safest temporary state (in force now):** a bank-reconciled payment is NOT reversible through this mechanism (refused, with a message pointing to the bank correction workflow) and remains immutable. NON-bank-reconciled cleared payments are fully reversible (the certified path).

## 2 · A dedicated reverse-only permission — DEFERRED (not necessary this session)

- **Operator instruction:** "Reversal must NOT be available merely because a user can edit a payment. Separate EDIT PAYMENT from REVERSE PAYMENT... Do not create a new permission namespace unless the existing permission model genuinely cannot represent reversal authority. If a dedicated permission is necessary, document why."
- **What S61 did:** reversal is separated from EDIT **structurally**, not by a distinct permission — it is a distinct governed command (`ReverseCustomerPayment`/`ReverseVendorPayment`) that mints a SEPARATE immutable record and never mutates the original; the edit door cannot reverse (a cleared payment's economic fields are already immutable, and there is no reverse-by-edit path). Its permission is `operations:manage` — the SAME the entire finance command family (`ClearCustomerPayment`, `PaySupplierInvoice`, `ReceiveCustomerPayment`, `IssueCustomerInvoice`) requires.
- **Why a distinct permission was NOT added:** the finance permission model has exactly `operations:read` / `operations:manage` — there is no `operations:approve`/`operations:reverse`. A reverse-only authority distinct from `operations:manage` would require EITHER a new `EnterprisePermission` value (a FROZEN `packages/shared` change → its own FG gate) OR the D8–D11 approval control-plane (materiality/roles/SoD) — both explicitly OUT OF S61 scope ("Do NOT introduce approval-hierarchy work"). Reusing `operations:manage` avoids inventing authority and a frozen change, consistent with every other finance command.
- **Honest bound (recorded, not fixed):** with `operations:manage` governing both, a user who can operate finance can both edit (draft) payments and reverse (cleared) payments. The separation is at the ACTION/PATH level (distinct command, immutable original, edit-cannot-reverse), not a finer reverse-only role. A finer role is a future item: a frozen `EnterprisePermission` addition (FG gate) or part of the D8–D11 control-plane.

## 3 · What is NOT blocked (implemented + certified this session)

Reversal of a cleared, non-bank-reconciled customer or vendor payment: a separate immutable reversal record, a compensating `${base}-REV` mirror that unwinds cash + AR/AP + realized FX at original amounts (the exact `decideLifecycle` revocation the void/soft-delete path already uses — no invented accounting), the invoice/bill re-opened, idempotent (at most one reversal per payment), the original payment + its journal preserved byte-identical, all through the governed command spine + a D6 delete boundary that refuses deleting a cleared payment and redirects to the reversal. FX reversal semantics are DEFINED (mirror everything at original amounts), so no FX STOP was hit.
